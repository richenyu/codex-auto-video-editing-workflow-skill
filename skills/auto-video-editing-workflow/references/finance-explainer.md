# Finance And Business Explainer Rules

Use this reference for finance, stock market, macro, business, AI startup, and technical explainer videos.

## Narrative Shape

A strong explainer usually follows:

```text
1. Hook: what happened or why it matters
2. Context: what changed
3. Mechanism: why it happened
4. Impact: who benefits, who is at risk, what changes
5. Viewpoint: the creator's direct judgment
6. Signals: what to watch next
7. Practical close: what the audience should not misunderstand
```

## Speech Cleanup For Finance

Be extra careful with:

- repeated ETF or stock lists;
- repeated market nouns such as "market, market";
- repeated futures/index words;
- misheard tickers;
- repeated company names;
- restarts before numbers;
- half-sentences before a complete point.

Keep:

- numbers;
- time frames;
- market direction;
- risk conditions;
- first/second/third structure;
- explicit viewpoint.

## Material Matching

Match materials to concrete nouns and claims:

- index / ETF / stock: chart, ticker, official finance page, market screen, or relevant company material;
- company: official website, product, building, chip/server/factory, or real footage;
- oil / inflation / rate: chart, commodity scene, Fed/treasury visual, or macro graphic;
- AI startup: product page, GitHub, demo recording, realistic office/lab/data center scene;
- local business: store, warehouse, school, recycle station, service shop, camera/security scene;
- analogy: cinematic but visually precise scene that supports the metaphor.

Do not use generic "AI data" visuals when the sentence names a specific object, company, product, place, market, or business process.

## Visual Mix

For a 2-minute business/finance/technical explainer:

- Aim for 5-7 real or video-like full-screen cutaways when enough strong materials exist.
- Use face shots for trust and direct viewpoint.
- Use large overlays for concise conclusions or watchlists.
- Use full-screen cutaways for concrete nouns, company names, examples, places, products, and analogies.
- Use charts sparingly and clearly.
- Avoid turning the whole edit into an animated slide deck.

## Material Quality

The first requirement is that the material looks good. The second is that it matches the sentence. Both must pass.

Reject:

- blurry screenshots;
- cluttered finance pages with unreadable small text;
- random stock-looking scenes;
- dark or ugly AI dashboards;
- generic English fake UI;
- repeated source assets;
- weak still-image motion;
- watermarked or obviously copied short-video material.

Prefer:

- official pages;
- clean screenshots;
- product demos;
- licensed or user-provided footage;
- realistic cinematic AI-generated scenes;
- screen recordings with cursor movement, scroll, highlights, and chart motion.

## BGM Mood

Finance/business BGM should feel:

- bright;
- confident;
- forward-moving;
- lightly inspirational;
- clean;
- money-making.

Avoid:

- negative-news mood;
- suspense trailer mood;
- gloomy corporate mood;
- heavy bass;
- oppressive tension.

## Compliance Note

This skill helps edit videos. It does not provide financial advice. When editing finance content, preserve uncertainty, risk language, and conditions. Do not visually imply certainty where the spoken content is conditional.

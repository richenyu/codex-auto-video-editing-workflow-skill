# Finance And Business Explainer Style

Use this reference for US stocks, macro, finance, business cases, company earnings, AI infrastructure, and market commentary.

## Core Direction

Learn the method of high-quality finance explainers:

- problem-first opening;
- story-like explanation;
- causal chain diagrams;
- data visualization;
- real or reference material shots;
- chaptered explanation;
- ending with an observation framework.

Do not copy another creator's actual footage, covers, music, animation package, channel packaging, or wording.

## Structure

Use this story shape when possible:

```text
1. Hook: a counterintuitive question or clear judgment.
2. Fact: what happened, with numbers, time, and object.
3. Explanation: why it happened, using causal chains.
4. Impact: who benefits and who is pressured.
5. Risk: what is not solved.
6. Framework: 2-4 signals to watch next.
7. Closing line: vivid analogy, but not a substitute for risk context.
```

## Material Priority

Preferred:

- self-made charts and simple dashboards;
- index, ETF, and company cards;
- oil, inflation, central-bank, and rate causal chains;
- yield or price trend cards;
- company, official, or public web pages;
- maps and policy or news context;
- checklists for "watch these signals".

Use sparingly:

- generic stock-market backgrounds;
- generic AI or finance illustrations;
- unrelated tech cards.

## Not Full Slide Deck

Finance explainer videos may contain slide-like cards, but the whole video must not become an animated slide deck.

Default approximate visual mix:

```text
Talking head / face: 35%-55%
Full-screen real cutaways / B-roll: 20%-35%
Feature or overlay materials: 15%-25%
Opinion or explanation cards: 5%-15%
```

When talking about a concrete object, cut to a concrete material:

- NVIDIA -> NVIDIA sign, GPU, data center, server, supply chain chart.
- Oracle -> Oracle page or sign, cloud and data center, earnings or order-to-profit chart.
- Oil and geopolitics -> oil chart, map, shipping or energy visual.
- Aircraft and travel -> airplane photo or video, airport, route, or map visual.
- Restaurant analogy -> full-screen queue, kitchen, store, cash register, or ledger visual.

## Cutaway Requirement

For mature finance and business explainers, full-screen cutaways are a core editing language, not an optional decoration.

- Use real or realistic materials whenever a concrete subject appears.
- Photos, GIFs, short videos, official screenshots, maps, and scene footage may fully cover the speaker.
- Keep the speaker's audio running under the cutaway.
- Do not reduce every material to a small floating card or half-screen panel.
- A finance or business explainer around 2 minutes should usually contain 5-7 real full-screen cutaways, with 6 as the default planning target when relevant material exists.
- A 60-180 second explainer should aim for about 3 real full-screen cutaways per minute; fewer than 2 should fail QA unless the user explicitly requested a face-only style.
- If a 2-minute finance explainer has almost no full-screen real cutaways, treat it as under-edited.
- If every full-screen visual is a slide card, treat it as still too slide-like.

## Strict Matching

- Disable semantic fallback when topic stages matter.
- Do not let closing cards appear in the middle.
- Bind stage materials to corresponding spoken stages.
- If a sentence has no matching material, use face or background blur.

For codebases that support it, use a "feature B-roll" bias for finance explainers so strong matches become large material shots instead of small overlays.
